📖 Operating Systems Concepts & Proofs

Comprehensive theoretical foundation, mathematical proofs, and empirical verification evidence for academic evaluation.

01

Threads & Concurrency (POSIX pthreads)

A thread is the smallest unit of CPU scheduling sharing a process address space. In our implementation:

  • Each of the 5 philosophers runs as an independent concurrent POSIX thread spawned with pthread_create().
  • Threads execute an event loop (Thinking → Hungry → Waiting → Eating) with responsive 20ms non-blocking sleeps checking cancellation flags.
  • All threads are joined cleanly with pthread_join() on engine stop, leaving zero orphaned processes.
pthread_create(&engine->philosophers[i].thread, NULL, philosopher_thread_func, args);
02

Synchronization & Mutual Exclusion

When concurrent threads access shared mutable state, synchronization primitives prevent race conditions and corruption:

  • POSIX Mutexes: Each fork $F_i$ is protected by a dedicated pthread_mutex_t. Only the philosopher holding the lock can access the fork.
  • Condition Variables: pthread_cond_t channels allow threads to sleep safely when resources are unavailable and wake up immediately upon release without busy-waiting.
  • State Mutex: state_mutex serializes metric updates and snapshot telemetry.
03

Critical Sections & Race Condition Prevention

A critical section is a sequence of code accessing shared resources that must not be executed concurrently by multiple threads:

  • Fork Ownership Section: Updating forks[i].owner_id and forks[i].is_available is strictly protected under that fork's mutex.
  • Admission Section: Evaluating whether a philosopher can dine under the Fair Monitor is atomic inside monitor_mutex.
  • Locks are never held during blocking I/O, ensuring maximum concurrency.
04

The Four Coffman Conditions for Deadlock

Deadlock can occur if and only if all four Coffman conditions (1971) hold simultaneously:

1. Mutual Exclusion: Forks are non-shareable resources.
2. Hold & Wait: A philosopher holding one fork can wait for the second.
3. No Preemption: Forks cannot be forcibly revoked.
4. Circular Wait: A closed chain $P_0 \to F_1 \to P_1 \to F_2 \to P_2 \to F_3 \to P_3 \to F_4 \to P_4 \to F_0 \to P_0$ forms.
05

Deadlock Prevention Proof (Resource Ordering)

Dijkstra's Hierarchy Theorem: Imposing a strict total order $F_0 \prec F_1 \prec F_2 \prec F_3 \prec F_4$ breaks circular wait.

Proof by Contradiction:
Suppose a circular wait cycle exists: $P_0 \to F_{a_1} \to P_1 \to \dots \to F_{a_0} \to P_0$.
Under resource ordering, every thread acquires $\min(L, R)$ before $\max(L, R)$. Hence, whenever a philosopher holds fork $x$ and waits for fork $y$, it is guaranteed that $x < y$.
Summing along the cycle yields: $\text{index}(F_{a_0}) < \text{index}(F_{a_1}) < \dots < \text{index}(F_{a_0}) \implies F_{a_0} < F_{a_0}$, which is a contradiction.
Hence, the Resource Allocation Graph is an Acyclic Directed Graph (DAG); deadlock is impossible. ▪
06

Starvation Prevention (Fair FIFO Monitor)

Deadlock prevention does not guarantee starvation-freedom. In our Fair FIFO Monitor:

  • Hungry philosophers are enqueued in an arrival queue (fifo_queue).
  • A philosopher is admitted if and only if both forks are free and no earlier queued philosopher requires either fork.
  • Under the finite eating duration assumption ($t_{eat} \le T_{max}$), any request at position $K$ in the queue waits at most $K \cdot T_{max}$ before admission, guaranteeing starvation-freedom.
